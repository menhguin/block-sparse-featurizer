Research question (be rigorous, cross-disciplinary, and adversarial):

THE PROBLEM. We select a VARIABLE-SIZE subset from a pool of candidates by thresholding a per-item score. The pool is large and fixed in size (~256 candidates) but the number of TRULY PRESENT items varies per input (a latent random variable / "true support"). We want ONE threshold rule that generalises across inputs AND across distribution shift WITHOUT per-input or per-dataset re-tuning — the selected count should automatically adapt to each input's latent complexity. The concrete instance is block selection in a sparse dictionary / overcomplete autoencoder (a "Block-Sparse Featurizer" for neural-network interpretability): for each input, ~256 candidate "blocks" each receive an L2 norm (a score); the stock method keeps a FIXED top-k (k=8) largest by norm. We are replacing fixed-k with a distribution-aware, per-input-adaptive threshold.

OUR EMPIRICAL FINDINGS (calibrate to these — the answer should engage them, not ignore them):
1. Inactive-item scores fit a chi distribution (degrees of freedom = block dimension, here 3) NEAR-PERFECTLY (Kolmogorov-Smirnov D = 0.0019). So a clean, well-specified NOISE NULL exists for the "off" items.
2. MAX-referenced thresholds (min-p sampling style: keep score >= p * max_score) work POORLY here, because the max-score item is often a generic/dominant/positional artifact rather than signal — the OPPOSITE of LLM logit sampling, where the max logit is the model's trustworthy best guess.
3. A collision-probability / participation-ratio threshold (the "p-less" sampler: threshold = sum of squared normalized scores = exp(-Renyi-2 entropy)) RANKS inputs by their true support best of all methods tried (Pearson corr 0.70 with ground-truth support) but its ABSOLUTE operating point is mis-scaled (over-selects ~2.5x).
4. Shannon-ENTROPY-of-scores ANTI-correlates with true support (corr -0.53) — entropy is NOT a proxy for "how many items are present" in this setting.
5. Gap/cliff/knee detection (truncate at the largest sorted-score gap) FAILS — the sorted score curve is a smooth continuum with no knee, at both aggregate and per-input level.

THE DEEP QUESTION. What is the theoretically PRINCIPLED way to set such a selection threshold — grounded in a NULL / NOISE MODEL so that the single free hyperparameter is a FALSE-POSITIVE / ERROR RATE (alpha), dimensionless and therefore transferable across inputs and distributions — rather than a benchmark-tuned magnitude or fraction? And critically: does such a null-referenced, variable-size selection rule PROVABLY GENERALISE across samples of differing, a-priori-unbounded/undetermined support size? Is this a solved problem in some field (and if so, what is the canonical method and its transfer guarantee), or is this framing novel?

COVER THESE FOUR EVIDENCE FRONTIERS EXPLICITLY:
(A) CLASSICAL STATISTICS of variable-size selection at controlled error rate: false discovery rate (Benjamini-Hochberg and descendants, Storey q-values), family-wise error, multiple-hypothesis testing, higher criticism (Donoho-Jin), universal / VisuShrink / SURE thresholding (Donoho-Johnstone wavelet shrinkage), Stein's unbiased risk estimate. How do these pick a VARIABLE number of "discoveries" via an alpha, and what are their cross-sample / cross-distribution guarantees?
(B) CONFORMAL PREDICTION and calibrated set-valued prediction: split/full conformal, adaptive prediction sets (APS/RAPS), Mondrian/class-conditional conformal, the exchangeability coverage guarantee, and known results on coverage under DISTRIBUTION SHIFT (weighted conformal, Tibshirani et al.). This may be the closest existing formalization of "one alpha that provably transfers across samples" — assess how directly it maps onto per-input variable-size subset selection.
(C) COMPRESSED SENSING / SPARSE CODING / DICTIONARY LEARNING: support recovery under unknown or varying sparsity level, model-order selection (MDL, BIC, AIC), Lasso / group-Lasso threshold selection, false-support-recovery control, stability selection (Meinshausen-Buhlmann). How is the sparsity level / support size chosen adaptively and does it transfer?
(D) LLM SAMPLING + SPARSE-AUTOENCODER / INTERPRETABILITY thresholding: min-p, top-nsigma, eta-sampling and the truncation-sampling lineage; JumpReLU SAEs, BatchTopK / TopK SAEs, threshold learning in SAEs. Has anyone applied a per-input adaptive false-positive-rate / noise-null threshold to dictionary codes or SAE latents (as opposed to a global learned threshold or a fixed k)? Flag any direct prior art on "adaptive-alpha per-input selection for sparse features."

ADVERSARIAL / DISCONFIRMATION REQUIREMENTS (mandatory):
- Actively search for PRIOR ART that already does exactly this (null-referenced variable-size selection with cross-sample transfer), especially in the SAE/interpretability and conformal literatures. If it exists, name it precisely and say our framing is NOT novel.
- Actively search for evidence AGAINST the approach: cases where noise-null-referenced or FDR-style or conformal thresholds FAIL to transfer under distribution shift, or where the noise-null (e.g. a chi/Gaussian assumption) breaks. State the conditions under which null-referenced thresholding is known to be non-robust.
