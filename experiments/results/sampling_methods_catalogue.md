# Catalogue: Distribution-Aware Truncation Sampling Methods (Min-p Lineage) and Their Block-Selection Analogues

**Purpose.** Map the family of adaptive/distribution-aware truncation-sampling methods for LLM decoding onto the analogous problem of **block selection in a Block-Sparse Featurizer (BSF)** — an interpretability method (Goodfire, arXiv:2606.25234) that decomposes vision-model activations into sparse sums of concept "blocks."

**The analogy.**
- LLM sampling: logits over vocab → truncation sampler picks WHICH TOKENS survive before sampling. `top-k` keeps a fixed count; min-p/top-nσ/etc. keep a variable count based on distribution shape.
- BSF: per-patch L2 norms over ~256 candidate blocks → selector decides WHICH BLOCKS are active. Stock BSF = block top-k (k=8 largest norms), a fixed count exactly analogous to LLM top-k.
- The problem min-p solves ("fixed truncation doesn't adapt to how peaked/flat the distribution is") is exactly our problem ("fixed block count doesn't adapt to how many concepts a patch actually contains").

**What we already know (frames the ratings below).**
- MAX-referenced selection (our `min-z` port: keep blocks with norm ≥ p·max_norm) performs **poorly** — the max-norm block is often a dominant, generic/positional block, not a trustworthy anchor. This is the *opposite* of LLM sampling, where the max logit is usually the model's best guess.
- NOISE-FLOOR-referenced selection (inactive-block norms fit a χ₃ distribution near-perfectly, KS-D = 0.0019; threshold = χ₃ noise ceiling) is empirically well supported.
- The target metric is **selection correctness / robustness across varying per-patch complexity**, not raw reconstruction R² (parity with top-k already achieved there). We weight portability toward entropy- and noise-floor-referenced methods, and toward feedback-controlled methods that could stabilize the hyperparameter across complexity regimes.

## 1. Method Catalogue

### 1.1 Top-k Sampling
**Paper:** Fan, Lewis, Dauphin, *Hierarchical Neural Story Generation*, 2018 (ACL).
**Core mechanism:** Keep the `k` tokens with the highest probability; renormalize and sample.
**Reference point:** None — fixed cardinality, no distribution-shape awareness.
**Key property:** Simple, cheap, temperature-invariant in *cardinality* (not in *content*, since which k tokens matters). Fails to adapt: too permissive on peaked distributions, too restrictive on flat ones.
**BSF analogue:** This *is* stock BSF (block top-k, k=8). No transplant needed — it's the baseline being improved on.
**Portability:** N/A (baseline).

### 1.2 Temperature Scaling
**Paper:** Ackley, Hinton, Sejnowski, *A Learning Algorithm for Boltzmann Machines*, 1985 (Cognitive Science). Standard LLM-decoding usage popularized by GPT-2/3 era work.
**Core mechanism:** Divide logits by `T` before softmax, sharpening (`T<1`) or flattening (`T>1`) the distribution. Not itself a truncation rule.
**Reference point:** None (global rescaling of the whole distribution).
**Key property:** Governs how "confident vs. flat" the distribution looks to *downstream* truncation rules — this is precisely why min-p and top-nσ care about temperature invariance.
**BSF analogue:** A global norm-rescaling factor (e.g., a learned or per-layer scalar applied to all block norms) — could sharpen/flatten per-patch norm profiles before thresholding. Not itself a selection rule; only useful in combination with a truncation method.
**Portability:** Low as a standalone idea — useful only as an auxiliary knob alongside a real selector.

### 1.3 Top-p / Nucleus Sampling
**Paper:** Holtzman, Buys, Du, Forbes, Choi, *The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration*, arXiv:1904.09751, ICLR 2020.
**Core mechanism:** Sort tokens by probability descending; keep the smallest prefix set whose cumulative probability mass exceeds threshold `p` (typically 0.9).
**Reference point:** Cumulative probability mass (an integral over the *sorted* distribution, not a pointwise/local statistic).
**Key property:** Adapts pool size to entropy in one specific sense (flatter distributions → larger nucleus), but is provably unstable at high temperature: as the distribution flattens, cumulative mass accrues over many low-probability (noise) tokens before hitting `p`, admitting garbage. Min-p and top-nσ were both built specifically to fix this failure mode.
**BSF analogue:** Sort blocks by norm descending, keep the smallest prefix whose cumulative *squared*-norm (≈ share of reconstructed energy) exceeds a threshold `p`. Failure mode: same as in LLMs — on "noisy"/high-entropy patches (many blocks with middling, non-negligible norms) this admits far too many blocks, since the threshold references *total mass*, not a noise/signal boundary.
**Portability:** Low. Cumulative-mass criteria don't know where "noise" starts; exactly the failure mode min-p and top-nσ target.

### 1.4 Min-p Sampling
**Paper:** Nguyen, Baker, Neo, Roush, Kirsch, Shwartz-Ziv, *Turning Up the Heat: Min-p Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM Outputs*, arXiv:2407.01082, ICLR 2025 (oral).
**Core mechanism:** `p_scaled = p_base × p_max`, where `p_max = max_v P(v|context)`. Keep `V_min = {v : P(v) ≥ p_scaled}`; renormalize and sample.
**Reference point:** **Max probability** (i.e., the model's top pick, used as a confidence proxy).
**Key property:** Threshold scales with model confidence — tight pool when confident, loose pool when uncertain — improving coherence/diversity balance at high temperature. Later shown (top-nσ paper) to be, under a uniform-logit assumption, equivalent to `top-(1-p)` — i.e. essentially a static logit-space cutoff below the max, not truly "dynamic" in the way originally framed.
**BSF analogue:** Exactly our `min-z`: keep blocks with `norm ≥ p × max_norm`. **Already tried — performs poorly.** Root cause: max-norm block in BSF is frequently a generic/positional block (present and large regardless of patch content), not "the model's best guess" the way `p_max` is trustworthy in LLM sampling. The anchor assumption that made min-p work (max = signal) inverts in our domain (max = noise/generic).
**Portability:** **Low** — direct negative evidence in our setting. Included for completeness/contrast, not as a candidate.

### 1.5 Top-a Sampling
**Paper:** No formal paper; community-originated sampler (documented in oobabooga/text-generation-webui, and surveyed alongside min-p in community write-ups e.g. Hellisotherpeople's "Conspiracy Against High Temperature Sampling" gist).
**Core mechanism:** Threshold = `α × p_max²` (squared max probability) rather than min-p's linear scaling.
**Reference point:** **Max probability**, squared — an even more aggressively confidence-anchored variant of min-p.
**Key property:** More aggressive truncation when confident, more permissive when uncertain — same idea as min-p but with a steeper (quadratic) response curve.
**BSF analogue:** `norm ≥ α × max_norm²` (dimensionally odd unless norms are pre-normalized to [0,1]; would need a normalized-norm variant). Inherits and *amplifies* min-p's failure mode since it's still max-anchored.
**Portability:** **Low** — same root problem as min-p, likely worse given the amplification.

### 1.6 ε-Sampling
**Paper:** Hewitt, Manning, Liang, *Truncation Sampling as Language Model Desmoothing*, arXiv:2210.15191, Findings of EMNLP 2022.
**Core mechanism:** Keep any token with absolute probability `P(v) > ε` for a fixed hyperparameter `ε` (e.g., ≈0.0009).
**Reference point:** **Absolute probability floor** — no adaptation to context at all (fixed threshold in probability space, closer in spirit to top-k than to min-p).
**Key property:** Framed via a "desmoothing" theory: language models are treated as a true distribution mixed with a smoothing/noise component; ε-sampling enforces the "absolute probability principle" — words below a fixed floor are assumed smoothing noise regardless of context. Over-truncates high-entropy (flat) distributions, under-truncates low-entropy ones — the mirror-image failure of top-p.
**BSF analogue:** Fixed absolute norm floor: keep blocks with `norm > ε` for some global constant. This is essentially a static magnitude gate — ignores per-patch complexity entirely, so it inherits ε-sampling's failure: over-truncates complex/high-entropy patches, permits too much on simple ones.
**Portability:** **Low** as a standalone idea, but conceptually a *component* worth keeping in combination (see η-sampling below) — a hard floor as a safety net under an adaptive threshold.

### 1.7 η-Sampling
**Paper:** Same as ε-sampling — Hewitt, Manning, Liang, arXiv:2210.15191.
**Core mechanism:** `η = min(ε, α·exp(−H))`, where `H = H(P_θ(·|context))` is the Shannon entropy of the current token distribution and `α = √ε` in the paper's default. Keep tokens with `P(v) > η`.
**Reference point:** **Entropy of the full distribution**, combined with an absolute floor `ε` as a safety cap. `exp(−H)` is the probability a token would have under a hypothetical *uniform* distribution with the same entropy — so the entropy-scaled term says "truncate relative to what a token 'should' get if mass were spread evenly at this entropy level."
**Key property:** Explicitly designed to respect both the "absolute probability principle" (don't keep vanishingly low-probability words) and the "relative probability principle" (adapt to how spread-out the distribution is) simultaneously — this is the closest architectural precursor to entropy-adaptive sampling as a formal category, and it directly motivates locally typical sampling and Mirostat's design goals.
**BSF analogue:** Compute Shannon entropy `H` of the (normalized) per-patch block-norm distribution; set threshold `η = min(ε_floor, α·exp(−H))` on *normalized* block-norm-shares. Complex/high-entropy patches (many real concepts) get a looser threshold; simple/low-entropy patches (few concepts) get a tighter one — this is exactly the "adapt to per-patch complexity" property we want, and unlike min-p it references entropy rather than the max.
**Portability:** **Medium–High.** Directly targets our stated problem (adapt to per-patch complexity, not the max). Caveat: entropy of the norm distribution requires a well-defined normalization step (softmax-over-norms or share-of-sum-of-squares) that doesn't exist natively in BSF the way it does for LLM logits — needs to be defined and validated as a meaningful proxy for "concept count" before use.

### 1.8 Locally Typical Sampling
**Paper:** Meister, Pimentel, Wiher, Cotterell, *Locally Typical Sampling*, arXiv:2202.00666, TACL 2023.
**Core mechanism:** Keep tokens whose negative log-probability (information content) is close to the conditional entropy `H(Y_t | Y_{<t})` of the model at that step — formally, solve a constrained optimization that selects the smallest set of tokens (near the entropy) covering probability mass `τ`.
**Reference point:** **Conditional entropy** — the threshold is not a boundary on probability magnitude at all, but a *band* around expected information content. Tokens with unusually high probability (too predictable) or unusually low probability (too surprising) are both excluded.
**Key property:** Motivated by the "uniform information density" hypothesis from psycholinguistics — human-like text keeps each word's surprisal close to the expected value. Reduces degenerate repetition better than nucleus/top-k. Explicitly compared by the authors to Mirostat (similar decision rule, but typical sampling derives its target from the *current* distribution's entropy rather than requiring a fixed target set externally).
**BSF analogue:** Keep blocks whose (normalized) `-log(norm_share)` is close to the entropy of the per-patch norm distribution — i.e., select "moderately-important" blocks and *exclude* the dominant block(s) as well as the noise tail. This is a genuinely different selection shape than a simple threshold: it's a *band*, not a one-sided cutoff. Interesting because it would automatically down-weight an overly-dominant generic block (addressing our max-block problem) while still filtering noise from below.
**Portability:** **Medium.** The double-sided band structure is conceptually attractive for suppressing the dominant-block problem, but it's a more complex selection rule to tune/justify than a one-sided threshold, and "typicality" as a concept (matching human information-rate expectations) has no obvious ground-truth analogue for block selection — the justification would need to be empirical/entropy-based rather than borrowed wholesale.

### 1.9 Mirostat
**Paper:** Basu, Ramachandran, Khuller, Deshpande (misattributed at times to Basu et al. 2020), *Mirostat: A Neural Text Decoding Algorithm that Directly Controls Perplexity*, arXiv:2007.14966, ICLR 2021.
**Core mechanism:** A **feedback-control** algorithm. Estimates the Zipf exponent `s` of the current distribution's tail from the top-2 token probabilities, uses it to compute the `k` that would be needed (under an assumed Zipfian model) to hit a *target* per-step surprisal `τ` (equivalently, target perplexity), applies top-k with that `k`, then measures the realized surprisal error and updates its internal estimate for the next step (a discrete PI-controller-like update).
**Reference point:** **A target perplexity/cross-entropy value, held constant via closed-loop feedback** — not a property of the current-step distribution alone; the reference is user-specified globally, and the controller adapts `k` at each step to hit it.
**Key property:** Directly controls a global output statistic (perplexity) regardless of local distribution shape — avoids both the "boredom trap" (perplexity too low → repetition) and "confusion trap" (perplexity too high → incoherence) without per-context hyperparameter search.
**BSF analogue:** Rather than a fixed noise-floor multiplier `k` (as in our `mean + k·σ_noise` rule), run a feedback loop across patches/images that adjusts `k` dynamically to hold a *target* aggregate statistic constant — e.g., target average active-block count, or target reconstruction-fidelity/entropy-of-selection across the complexity spectrum — correcting the threshold multiplier based on the realized statistic on recent patches. This could directly address a known failure mode of *any* fixed-threshold method: a single global `p` or `k`-multiplier that's well-tuned for average-complexity patches will be miscalibrated for both very simple and very complex ones.
**Portability:** **High** — not as a replacement selection rule, but as a *meta-mechanism* layered on top of a noise-floor selector to solve the "stable hyperparameter across varying complexity" problem, which is explicitly one of our stated goals.

### 1.10 Contrastive Decoding — Adaptive Plausibility Constraint
**Paper:** Li, Holtzman, Fried, Liang, Eisner, Hashimoto, Zettlemoyer, Lewis, *Contrastive Decoding: Open-ended Text Generation as Optimization*, arXiv:2210.15097, ACL 2023.
**Core mechanism:** `V_head(x_{<i}) = {v : p_EXP(v) ≥ α·max_w p_EXP(w)}` — restrict the candidate pool by the *expert* model's own max-probability-scaled threshold (identical algebraic form to min-p, published independently and earlier — min-p's authors note this explicitly), then re-score surviving candidates by `log p_EXP(v) − log p_AMA(v)` (expert-vs-amateur contrast) rather than by raw probability.
**Reference point:** **Max probability** (of the expert model) for the truncation step; a **second model's log-probability** for the re-scoring step.
**Key property:** Truncation and scoring are decoupled — the max-referenced constraint exists purely to prevent the contrastive *objective* from selecting implausible-but-high-contrast tokens; it is not claimed to be an adaptive-complexity mechanism in its own right.
**BSF analogue:** Same as min-p/min-z for the truncation half (max-norm-scaled threshold) — inherits the same known failure mode. The interesting transplantable idea is the *second* half: re-score/re-rank surviving blocks by a contrast between two different measurements (e.g., a small vs. large/simple vs. complex encoder, or current-layer vs. earlier-layer norms) rather than raw norm — see DoLa below for the more developed version of this idea.
**Portability:** **Low** for the truncation mechanism itself (same failure mode as min-p); the contrastive re-scoring idea is a different, potentially useful, orthogonal concept but requires a second "amateur" reference model/representation we don't currently have in the BSF pipeline.

### 1.11 DoLa (Decoding by Contrasting Layers)
**Paper:** Chuang, Xie, Luo, Kim, Glass, He, *DoLa: Decoding by Contrasting Layers Improves Factuality in Large Language Models*, arXiv:2309.03883, ICLR 2024.
**Core mechanism:** Instead of truncating a single distribution, DoLa contrasts the *final* ("mature") layer's projected logit distribution against an earlier ("premature") layer's, selected dynamically per-token by choosing whichever earlier layer maximizes Jensen-Shannon divergence from the mature layer (within a pre-selected bucket of candidate layers). The next-token distribution becomes `log p_mature − log p_premature` (softmaxed).
**Reference point:** **An internally-generated background/reference distribution** (an earlier layer's belief), chosen per-step by *maximum divergence* from the final layer — not a fixed statistic like max or entropy, but a dynamically selected internal contrast target.
**Key property:** Surfaces factual knowledge believed to be "finalized" only in later layers, by explicitly downweighting what's already known/predictable from earlier layers. Requires no extra model — reuses the same network's intermediate representations.
**BSF analogue:** If the BSF or its encoder has multiple layers/stages, contrast final-layer block norms against an earlier-layer's block-norm profile (or against a rolling "expected generic profile" computed from many patches) — i.e., treat "what an early/generic pass already explains" as the *background/noise reference*, and select blocks whose norm is anomalously large relative to that background rather than relative to the global max. This is conceptually close to our noise-floor approach but makes the "noise reference" *input-conditioned and layer-derived* rather than a single global χ₃ fit — worth exploring if BSF has any multi-stage/hierarchical structure to exploit.
**Portability:** **Medium** — the mechanism (contrast against a dynamically chosen background reference rather than the max) is exactly the right shape for our problem, but it requires an available second "premature" representation (an earlier encoder stage, or a lower-capacity/generic-patch model) that may not currently exist in the BSF pipeline as an off-the-shelf artifact.

### 1.12 Entmax / Sparsemax Sampling
**Papers:** Martins, Astudillo, *From Softmax to Sparsemax*, ICML 2016; Peters, Niculae, Martins, *Sparse Sequence-to-Sequence Models* (α-entmax), arXiv:1905.05702, ACL 2019; Martins, Marinho, Martins, *Sparse Text Generation*, arXiv:2004.02644, EMNLP 2020.
**Core mechanism:** Replace softmax with the `α-entmax` transformation (`α > 1`), a member of the Tsallis-entropy family that natively produces exactly-zero probabilities for low-scoring logits, with the sparsity pattern determined by solving a projection/normalization problem, not by an externally-set threshold. The model is *trained* with the corresponding Fenchel-Young loss so sparsity is learned end-to-end rather than imposed post hoc.
**Reference point:** **None externally set** — the implicit threshold is a Lagrange-multiplier-like normalization constant τ solved for at each step by the entmax projection itself; the "reference" is the entire logit vector's shape, filtered through the α-parameterized generalized-entropy transform.
**Key property:** Eliminates the train/test mismatch that all *post-hoc* truncation methods have (the model is never trained to expect its full softmax mass to be sampled from truthfully). Number of surviving tokens varies by context "natively," with more variability than nucleus sampling in the paper's experiments.
**BSF analogue:** Would require training (or fine-tuning) the BSF encoder itself with a sparse `α-entmax`-style normalization over block scores instead of an independent post-hoc threshold — i.e., make block-sparsity a trained property of the encoder rather than a decision made at inference by a separate selector. This is architecturally a much bigger lift than any other method here (all others are inference-time drop-in selectors); it's a training-time reformulation.
**Portability:** **Low** for a drop-in port (wrong intervention point — training vs. inference), but **conceptually High** as a longer-term research direction if the goal shifts from "best post-hoc selector" to "make the encoder natively sparse." Flag as out-of-scope for immediate experiments but worth remembering.

### 1.13 Contrastive Search (SimCTG)
**Paper:** Su, Lan, Wang, Yogatama, Kong, Collier, *A Contrastive Framework for Neural Text Generation*, arXiv:2202.06417, NeurIPS 2022.
**Core mechanism:** Restrict candidates to a small, *fixed* top-k (k≈3–10) by probability, then re-rank by `(1−α)·p_θ(v) − α·max_j sim(h_v, h_{x_j})` — a fixed-weight blend of model confidence and a "degeneration penalty" (max cosine similarity to any previously generated token's hidden state).
**Reference point:** **Fixed top-k** for the candidate pool (no adaptivity there); the *scoring* step references the **max similarity to prior context**, not the current-step distribution's shape.
**Key property:** Precursor to Adaptive Contrastive Search (below); demonstrates that penalizing "similarity to what's already been said" reduces repetition even under maximization-style (non-stochastic) decoding.
**BSF analogue:** Use a fixed small candidate pool (e.g., top-16 by norm) then re-rank by penalizing blocks whose activation pattern is highly similar to blocks already "used" elsewhere in the same image/scene (a redundancy penalty) rather than pure norm. Not directly about per-patch complexity adaptivity — it's about cross-patch/cross-token diversity, a different axis than our problem.
**Portability:** **Low** for our stated problem (per-input complexity adaptivity); the fixed-k front-end inherits the standard top-k limitation, and the degeneration-penalty idea addresses redundancy, not variable concept-count. Included for completeness as the direct ancestor of ACS.

### 1.14 Top-nσ
**Paper:** Tang et al. (exact author list per ACL Anthology: authored under "Top-nσ" team), *Top-nσ: Not All Logits Are You Need* / *Top-nσ: Eliminating Noise in Logit Space for Robust Token Sampling of LLM*, arXiv:2411.07641, ACL 2025 (long paper).
**Core mechanism:** Operates directly on **pre-softmax logits**. Computes `M = max(logits)` and `σ = std(logits)` over the *entire* vocabulary, keeps only logits with `l_i > M − n·σ` (masking the rest to `−∞`), then applies temperature and softmax. Default `n ≈ 1.0`.
**Reference point:** **Hybrid** — anchor is the max logit `M` (inherited from min-p's framing, and the paper explicitly proves min-p ≡ top-(1−p) under a uniform-logit assumption, motivating a logit-space reformulation), but the *scale* of the cutoff is the **global standard deviation of all logits**, which the paper argues is dominated by the bulk Gaussian noise cluster of uninformative tokens (empirically: logits separate into a Gaussian "noise" component plus a small number of outlier "informative" tokens). So the width of the window is noise-referenced even though the anchor point is max-referenced.
**Key property:** Provably temperature-invariant (both `M` and `σ` scale by `1/T` under temperature scaling, so `n` and the resulting nucleus are unaffected) — directly fixes top-p/min-p's temperature instability. Requires no sorting or softmax for the filtering step, only a mean/std computation.
**BSF analogue:** Two separable pieces: (a) the **noise-scale estimate** `σ` (or, better, our fitted χ₃ noise model) is directly transplantable and is in fact very close to what we already do; (b) the **max-anchoring** of the window (`M − nσ`) is the questionable part, given our evidence that the max block is untrustworthy. A cleaner BSF port drops the max anchor and instead thresholds directly from the noise-floor side: `norm > noise_ceiling(χ₃, k)`, i.e., exactly our existing method, but justified/cross-checked via top-nσ's "Gaussian-vs-outlier separation in raw (pre-normalization) score space" framing rather than assuming a parametric χ distribution a priori. Useful as an alternate/nonparametric noise-scale estimator (global empirical std of *inactive* block norms) to sanity-check the χ₃ fit, especially if group size or block-norm statistics change across settings.
**Portability:** **High** for the noise-scale-estimation half (this is essentially independent confirmation of our existing noise-floor approach from a different literature); **Low** for the max-anchoring half (same issue as min-p). Recommend explicitly decoupling anchor from scale when porting.

### 1.15 Adaptive Contrastive Search (ACS)
**Paper:** Garces Arias, Rodemann, Li, Heumann, Aßenmacher, *Adaptive Contrastive Search: Uncertainty-Guided Decoding for Open-Ended Text Generation*, arXiv:2407.18698, Findings of EMNLP 2024.
**Core mechanism:** Extends contrastive search by making both the candidate-pool size `k_t` and the confidence/penalty blend weight `α_t` *functions of Shannon entropy* at each step: entropy `H(X)^(t)` is centered against the running median of recent-step entropies and scaled by the maximum possible entropy (`ln|V|` or `ln k`), passed through `arctanh` and then a sigmoid, yielding continuous, bounded, per-step values for `k_t` and `α_t`.
**Reference point:** **Entropy, normalized relative to its own recent history (median) and to a theoretical maximum** — an explicitly *relative* (not absolute) entropy signal, framed by the authors as analogous to a statistical signal-to-noise-driven regularization parameter (they draw an explicit Tikhonov-regularization analogy: more "noise" in the model's output ⇒ larger regularization/penalty, i.e., larger effective pool and more contrastive weighting).
**Key property:** No manual hyperparameter tuning of `k`/`α` needed; the paper frames this directly as a signal-to-noise argument — the more uncertain (higher-entropy) the step, the more the pool should widen and the more the contrastive de-biasing should apply.
**BSF analogue:** Compute per-patch entropy of the (normalized) block-norm distribution, center it against a running median/baseline over recently-seen patches (or over a batch/dataset), and use the sigmoid-mapped result to continuously set the *number of active blocks* (in place of fixed k=8) or the noise-floor multiplier — giving a smooth per-patch adaptive parameter rather than a hard global threshold. The regularization framing (more entropy ⇒ trust the norms less, keep more candidates) maps cleanly onto "more concept-count uncertainty ⇒ keep more blocks."
**Portability:** **Medium–High.** Directly entropy-referenced (fits our stated preference), gives a principled continuous mapping rather than a hard cutoff, and its regularization-theoretic motivation (signal-to-noise ⇒ optimal shrinkage parameter) is a good match to "adapt threshold aggressiveness to per-patch noise level." Caveat: adds two coupled hyperparameters (the sigmoid's temperature `q`, and the entropy-normalization scheme) that need re-validation in norm-space rather than probability-space.

### 1.16 GUARD (Glocal Uncertainty-Aware Robust Decoding)
**Paper:** Ding et al., *GUARD: Glocal Uncertainty-Aware Robust Decoding for Effective and Efficient Open-Ended Text Generation*, arXiv:2508.20757, Findings of EMNLP 2025.
**Core mechanism:** Combines a **local** (current-step) entropy estimate with a **global** (running/aggregate, across the sequence generated so far) entropy estimate into a single "glocal" uncertainty signal, which is then used to adaptively tune contrastive-search-style parameters (in the spirit of ACS, but explicitly blending both timescales rather than only the local step-wise entropy).
**Reference point:** **Entropy, at two timescales (local + global) combined.**
**Key property:** Claimed to improve robustness and efficiency over purely local uncertainty signals (like ACS) by preventing overreaction to single noisy steps and underreaction to sustained regime shifts.
**BSF analogue:** Blend per-patch entropy (local) with a running estimate of "typical" entropy across the image or across a batch of images (global) to set the threshold/active-block-count — this directly targets "robustness across varying complexity," since a purely per-patch (local-only) signal can itself be noisy for a single patch, whereas an image-level or dataset-level running baseline provides a more stable reference for what counts as "high complexity" in context.
**Portability:** **Medium–High.** Precisely targets our stated robustness goal (varying per-input complexity) by explicitly guarding against over-fitting the threshold to single-patch noise. Requires deciding what the "global" timescale should be for a vision setting (per-image? per-dataset? per-layer?) — this design choice doesn't have a direct LLM-sequence analogue and needs its own validation.

### 1.17 REAL Sampling (Residual Entropy from Asymptotic Line)
**Paper:** Chang, Peng, Bansal, Ramakrishna, Chung, *REAL Sampling: Boosting Factuality and Diversity of Open-Ended Generation via Asymptotic Entropy*, arXiv:2406.07735; journal version *...by Extrapolating the Entropy of an Infinitely Large LM*, TACL 2025.
**Core mechanism:** Trains a small "Token-level Hallucination Forecasting" (THF) model that predicts the **asymptotic entropy** — the entropy an infinitely large LM would have at this step — by extrapolating next-token entropies observed across a family of LMs of increasing size. At generation time, if the *actual* model's entropy exceeds this predicted asymptotic (irreducible) floor, that excess signals elevated hallucination risk, and the nucleus-sampling threshold `p` is lowered; if entropy is at/below the floor, `p` is raised for more diversity.
**Reference point:** **A learned, input-conditioned noise/uncertainty floor** — explicitly the entropy floor a perfect/infinite model would still have (the theoretically irreducible aleatoric uncertainty), estimated by extrapolation across model scale. This is conceptually the most sophisticated "noise floor" framing in the whole lineage: not a fixed global constant, but a *predicted, context-dependent* floor.
**Key property:** Directly and explicitly frames "how far is the model's current uncertainty above the floor it *should* have" as the decision signal — outperforms plain nucleus/min-p-style baselines on a factuality-diversity tradeoff in the paper's benchmarks.
**BSF analogue:** Our current χ₃ noise-floor model is *global* (one noise distribution fit across all inactive blocks, presumably pooled over the dataset). REAL's idea suggests making the noise floor **input-conditioned**: train a small auxiliary model to predict, per patch (based on patch features, e.g., local image complexity/texture statistics), what the expected inactive-block-norm distribution *should* be for a patch of that apparent complexity — then threshold against that *predicted* floor rather than a single global χ₃ fit. This would let the noise ceiling itself flex with patch complexity instead of only the multiplier.
**Portability:** **High** as a conceptual upgrade path — it's the most directly analogous "noise-floor" method in the entire list, and offers a concrete mechanism (small auxiliary predictor, extrapolation-style) for making our already-successful noise-floor threshold context-adaptive rather than global-constant. Implementation cost is non-trivial (need a forecasting model and multiple "model sizes"/proxies to extrapolate from — the vision-model equivalent of "a family of LMs of different capacity" would need to be defined, e.g., different encoder capacities or different masking levels).

### 1.18 EDT (Entropy-based Dynamic Temperature)
**Paper:** *EDT: Improving Large Language Models' Generation by Entropy-based Dynamic Temperature Sampling*, arXiv:2403.14541, 2024.
**Core mechanism:** `T = T_0 · N^(θ/Entropy)` for constants `T_0, N (0<N<1), θ`; i.e., temperature is set as a decreasing function of current-step entropy, computed from a single forward pass (cheaper than the two-model KL-divergence-guided baseline it improves on).
**Reference point:** **Entropy of the current step's distribution**, mapped through a fixed parametric curve to a temperature multiplier (not itself a truncation rule, but reshapes the distribution before any downstream truncation is applied).
**Key property:** Single-model, lightweight, and empirically better-balanced (quality/diversity) than both fixed temperature and an earlier two-model KL-divergence-guided dynamic-temperature baseline.
**BSF analogue:** Rescale per-patch block norms (e.g., via a power/exponent applied before thresholding) as a function of that patch's own entropy — sharpen the norm profile on low-entropy (simple, low-concept-count) patches and flatten it on high-entropy (complex) ones, prior to applying a fixed selector. This is an auxiliary reshaping step, not a selector itself, analogous to temperature (§1.2) but made entropy-adaptive.
**Portability:** **Medium** — a plausible complement to any of the entropy- or noise-floor-referenced selectors above (apply before thresholding), but not itself a complete block-selection rule, and its specific parametric form (`N^(θ/Entropy)`) was tuned for LLM logit statistics and would need re-fitting for norm-space.

### 1.19 XTC (Exclude Top Choices)
**Source:** Community-originated sampler, first formalized by p-e-w in oobabooga/text-generation-webui PR #6335 (2024); adopted into llama.cpp (PR #9742) and other inference stacks. No peer-reviewed paper.
**Core mechanism:** Inverts the usual truncation logic. Finds all tokens with probability above a threshold `xtc_threshold` and below an upper threshold `xtc_threshold_max`; with probability `xtc_probability`, removes **all but the single least-probable member** of that high-probability cluster from the candidate pool (leaving at least one "safe" choice untouched for coherence).
**Reference point:** **A probability band near the top of the distribution** — the opposite reference direction from every other method above: instead of asking "how far below the max is still trustworthy," it asks "is the *max cluster itself* untrustworthy (boring/clichéd) and should be suppressed?"
**Key property:** Explicitly designed to fight the failure mode where the most probable tokens are the *problem* (clichés, repetition, predictable continuations) rather than the solution — a direct admission that "most probable ≠ most desirable" in some regimes, which is unusual among truncation methods (most treat "most probable" as ground truth to protect).
**BSF analogue:** This is arguably the most conceptually important entry for us, precisely because our evidence shows the max-norm block is *often exactly the "boring/generic" case XTC targets* (a dominant positional/generic block). An "XTC-block" rule would: identify when the top block(s) look "generic" by some independent signal (e.g., the block's norm is high on nearly *every* patch regardless of content — a genericness/low-selectivity signal, analogous to how XTC targets clichéd, over-used tokens) and *demote or exclude* that block from the active set even though it has the highest norm, while retaining the noise-floor-passing blocks below it. Unlike the noise-floor methods, this operates from the top of the distribution downward rather than the bottom upward, and could be used *in combination with* a noise-floor selector (noise floor sets the lower boundary; XTC-style exclusion polices the upper boundary against generic dominance).
**Portability:** **High as a complementary mechanism** (not a replacement for noise-floor thresholding, but a structurally different additional filter that directly targets the specific failure mode we've already diagnosed — an untrustworthy dominant block). Needs a genericness signal to operationalize (e.g., cross-patch frequency of a block being the argmax, or its correlation with position rather than content) which doesn't have a ready-made LLM equivalent (LLMs don't have an analogous "which token is highest most of the time regardless of context" signal computed at serve time) — this makes it a promising but non-trivial-to-implement idea, not a drop-in port.

### 1.20 "Min-k" / Semantic-Cliff Decay-Rate Sampling
**Paper:** *Sampling: Decoupling Truncation from Temperature Scaling via Relative Logit Dynamics* (Min-k Sampling), ACL 2026 (per ACL Anthology ID 2026.acl-long.681 — very recent, published after top-nσ, explicitly positioned as a response to top-nσ's limitations).
**Core mechanism:** Analyzes the **local shape of the sorted logit sequence** to detect a "semantic cliff" — a sharp local transition from a high-confidence core to an uncertain long tail — via a position-weighted relative logit decay rate, rather than a global statistic. Truncation boundary `k` is determined per-step from this local-curvature signal.
**Reference point:** **Local decay-rate / discontinuity in the sorted score sequence** — explicitly *not* the global max, *not* a global standard deviation (the paper's stated critique of top-nσ is that a single global `σ` is "susceptible to interference from background noise within the long-tail," i.e., a few outlier noise tokens can distort the global std estimate), and *not* raw entropy — a genuinely distinct fourth reference-point category (local curvature/gap detection) alongside max-referenced, cumulative-mass-referenced, and entropy/noise-floor-referenced methods.
**Key property:** Claims strict temperature invariance (like top-nσ) while being more locally robust to long-tail noise contamination of the threshold estimate, and low sensitivity to its own hyperparameters.
**BSF analogue:** Sort block norms descending and look for the sharpest relative drop-off ("cliff") in the sorted sequence — a per-patch, shape-based (not magnitude-anchored) cutoff detection. This is attractive because it requires no assumption about a global noise distribution (robust if the χ₃ noise model doesn't transfer to a new block-group size or a different encoder) and no max-anchoring (avoids the min-p failure mode) — it instead asks "where does the sorted-norm curve bend," which could in principle detect the true signal/noise boundary per patch even if the *absolute* noise level itself varies patch-to-patch (e.g., due to lighting/texture differences we haven't modeled).
**Portability:** **Medium–High**, and genuinely complementary/novel relative to everything else in this list (a fourth reference-point family: local curvature). Caveat: this is a very recent (2026), not-yet-widely-validated method; the "cliff detection" statistic itself (position-weighted relative decay) needs to be precisely reverse-engineered from the paper before porting, and gap/knee-detection heuristics are historically finicky (sensitive to noise in the sorted sequence itself, ironically) — worth prototyping but with lower confidence than the noise-floor/entropy families, which have deeper track records.

## 2. Ranked Shortlist — Most Promising Ports for BSF Block Selection

Ordered by expected value for our specific goal (**selection robustness across varying per-patch complexity**), not by general LLM-literature prominence.

| Rank | Method | Reference-point class | Why it's promising here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Our existing χ₃ noise-floor rule**, cross-validated against **Top-nσ's noise-scale estimation** (§1.14) | **NOISE-FLOOR** | Already empirically validated (KS-D=0.0019) and independently reinvented in the LLM literature via a different route (Gaussian-vs-outlier logit separation). Top-nσ's global-σ estimator is a useful non-parametric sanity check/fallback if the χ₃ assumption ever breaks (e.g., different group size). Recommend explicitly *dropping* top-nσ's max-anchor and keeping only its noise-scale-estimation logic. |
| **2** | **REAL Sampling's asymptotic-entropy-floor concept** (§1.17) | **NOISE-FLOOR** (learned, input-conditioned) | The most sophisticated noise-floor framing available: makes the "floor" a *predicted, per-input* quantity rather than a single global constant. Directly suggests the next iteration of our own method — an input-conditioned noise ceiling instead of one global χ₃ fit. Highest conceptual upside, moderate implementation cost. |
| **3** | **Mirostat-style feedback control** (§1.9) | **FEEDBACK / target-referenced** | Solves a problem our own noise-floor method likely still has: a single global multiplier `k` (in `mean + k·σ_noise`) is probably miscalibrated for both very-simple and very-complex patches. A closed-loop controller targeting a stable aggregate statistic (e.g., target average active-block count or reconstruction-fidelity) could stabilize the hyperparameter exactly the way Mirostat stabilizes perplexity — directly answers "fix hyperparameter instability," one of our explicit asks. |
| **4** | **η-Sampling's dual floor+entropy threshold** (§1.7) | **ENTROPY** (+ absolute floor) | Cleanest existing formalization of "adapt threshold to entropy while keeping a hard safety floor." Directly portable once per-patch block-norm entropy is defined; the dual-threshold (min of floor and entropy-scaled term) structure maps well onto combining with our noise floor as the "floor" term. |
| **5** | **ACS / GUARD entropy-normalized continuous parameterization** (§1.15, §1.16) | **ENTROPY** (local + local/global blend) | Turns a hard threshold into a smooth, per-patch continuous function of entropy — good for avoiding brittle cutoffs, and GUARD's local+global blend directly targets "robustness across varying complexity" by preventing over-reaction to single-patch noise. Slightly more machinery (running baselines, sigmoid mappings) than η-sampling. |
| **6** | **XTC-style dominant-block exclusion** (§1.19) | **Orthogonal / genericness-referenced** (neither max-trusting nor noise-floor) | Not a replacement for the above — a complementary upper-boundary filter that directly targets our diagnosed failure mode (max block = untrustworthy generic block). Needs a genericness signal not yet defined in our pipeline; higher implementation risk but directly addresses evidence we already have, unlike anything else on this list. |
| **7** | **Min-k / semantic-cliff local-curvature detection** (§1.20) | **LOCAL CURVATURE** (novel 4th category — neither max, mass, nor entropy) | Newest method (2026), unproven at scale, but conceptually attractive as a shape-based cutoff that needs neither a max anchor nor a fitted global noise distribution — worth a cheap prototype as a hedge against the χ₃ assumption not generalizing. |

### Explicit ENTROPY / NOISE-FLOOR vs. MAX flags

- **NOISE-FLOOR-referenced** (fits "robustness across complexity" thesis best): our χ₃ rule, Top-nσ's noise-scale half (§1.14), REAL Sampling (§1.17).
- **ENTROPY-referenced**: η-sampling (§1.7), locally typical sampling (§1.8, band around entropy), ACS (§1.15), GUARD (§1.16), EDT (§1.18, reshapes rather than truncates).
- **FEEDBACK/target-referenced** (neither pure entropy nor pure noise-floor, but directly useful for hyperparameter stability): Mirostat (§1.9).
- **MAX-referenced — de-weighted, evidence of poor fit in our setting**: min-p/min-z (§1.4), top-a (§1.5), contrastive decoding's adaptive plausibility constraint (§1.10), and the anchor (not scale) component of top-nσ (§1.14).
- **CUMULATIVE-MASS-referenced — also de-weighted** (different failure mode, doesn't know where noise starts): top-p/nucleus (§1.3).
- **Structurally orthogonal, worth tracking separately**: XTC (§1.19, targets dominant-block untrustworthiness directly), Min-k/semantic-cliff (§1.20, local curvature, a fourth reference-point family), DoLa (§1.11, contrast against a dynamically-selected internal background rather than a fixed statistic), entmax/sparsemax (§1.12, training-time not inference-time — longer-horizon idea).
